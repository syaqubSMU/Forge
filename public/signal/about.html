About the project

What FORGE SIGNAL is, and what it is not

AI does the fast first pass. People make the calls. SIGNAL helps research teams find funding and the right faculty, with clear sources and no invented data.

How it works

Two ways in, one careful process

Research development staff usually start in one of two places. Both end the same way: a person reviews the evidence and decides what goes out.

Path A · Faculty first

A PI asks, "What is coming up for me?"

SIGNAL starts from a verified faculty profile and screens open and forecasted opportunities in the PI's time window.

Path B · Opportunity first

A new funding opportunity appears

SIGNAL reads the sponsor announcement and screens verified faculty profiles for departments and people who may fit.

  1. Verify scopeFaculty profile quoted from Dedman pages, with a retrieved date.
  2. SummarizeAI drafts a fact sheet of priorities, eligibility and deadlines, each cited.
  3. ScreenEvidence-based match: High, Medium or Low, with the quotes shown.
  4. ReviewResearch Development confirms ratings, eligibility and policy.
  5. Reach outA person edits and sends the alert. Nothing is automatic.

AI & human collaboration

Each does what it does best

These boundaries come from the 2026 SMU Technology Showcase and govern every SIGNAL workflow.

Where AI helps most

Speed, volume and first-pass analysis, where speed is of the essence.

  • Rapid synthesis: summarizes NOFOs, policies, reports, accreditation standards
  • Drafting & structuring: first drafts of emails, summaries, tables, briefing materials
  • Pattern identification: highlights themes in qualitative data
  • Consistency & standardization: applies shared language across reports and communications
  • Targeted analysis: supports opportunity–faculty matching and tailored outreach
  • Early-stage sensemaking: helps frame questions and surface options quickly

Where human judgment is required

To provide trust, context and accountability.

  • Final interpretation & decisions: strategic choices, prioritization and trade-offs
  • Policy, eligibility & compliance: grant rules, accreditation standards, legal and financial risk
  • Equity & ethical considerations: assessing impact on underrepresented or vulnerable groups
  • Context & institutional knowledge: culture, history, politics and informal constraints
  • Relationship-based work: faculty engagement, leadership communication, trust-building
  • Accountability & defensibility: owning outcomes in audits, reviews and public settings

Humans are essential for setting the oversight, validation of information and direction, and ethical guardrails.

Ground rules

How SIGNAL keeps those boundaries

  • Nothing is sent for youSIGNAL never sends, submits or commits anyone to anything. Every message is edited and sent by a person.
  • Nothing is inventedNo made-up deadlines, eligibility rules, award amounts or faculty interests. Missing stays "Not stated."
  • Evidence you can checkEvery match shows its quote, source link and retrieved date, or says "No explicit support found in the provided sources."
  • Eligibility is never assumedIt starts as "Unclear: verify," and only a person can mark it clear. High ratings need a verified profile and confirmed eligibility.
  • People review who is surfacedResearch Development checks who an alert reaches, and who it misses, before it goes out.
  • No restricted dataPublic and Private data only, per SMU data governance. No student, health or financial records.

Who it serves

Built for research teams

SIGNAL v0.1 is a lightweight, public grant discovery application. It indexes a limited, clearly disclosed set of U.S. federal funding opportunity records, with links to the official announcement.

Research administrators

Find opportunities to share with faculty using broad search, sponsor filters, deadlines and official links.

Faculty researchers

Discover opportunities by topic with readable details and a saved list.

Department administrators

Track upcoming deadlines and revisit a chosen shortlist.

Research development staff

Scan across sponsors and disciplines with multiple filters, visible provenance and the matching Workbench.

Trust & coverage

What you can rely on, and where it stops

Core capabilities in v0.1

  • Discover and search: by title, synopsis, sponsor, opportunity number and program metadata.
  • Filter: sponsor, research area, status, funding instrument and deadline, with a default 30-day minimum lead time users can change or remove.
  • Inspect: structured details with unknown fields shown as "Not specified", plus a prominent link to the official announcement.
  • Save: no account needed. Saves live in the browser's local storage, so they are specific to that browser and device.

Trust and data integrity

  • Primary source is the public Grants.gov opportunity API, called from the server, never from the browser.
  • Every record carries its source system, source ID, source link and last-verified time, or an explicit missing state.
  • A failed sync never overwrites valid cached data, and the last successful refresh time is always shown.

SIGNAL currently indexes selected official federal sources. Always verify requirements in the sponsor announcement. It does not claim to list all federal funding opportunities. NSF opportunities will be validated against NSF's official Funding Search using a test set of at least 20 active records; where Grants.gov coverage has material gaps, SIGNAL will link out to NSF rather than imply complete coverage.

Architecture

What is planned

A Next.js and React front end, a Python FastAPI backend, a PostgreSQL database with full-text search, and a scheduled Python worker that syncs and normalizes Grants.gov records while retaining the raw payloads. The Workbench is a browser-only prototype today; any hosted version would go through accessibility, SSO, data-protection and OIT review first. Browse the live demo on the home page; it uses fictional sample data only.
