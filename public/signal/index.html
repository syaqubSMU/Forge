Research development

Find the right funding signal

SIGNAL reads current funding announcements, screens them against a researcher’s established work, and shows the sentence behind every claim. It builds a deadline calendar and drafts an alert. A person decides what goes out.

The problem

Too many announcements, too little time

Finding announcements is not the hard part. The burden is vigilance: programs and keywords keep changing, each announcement is a long document to compare with a researcher’s expertise, and deadlines do not move.

Scattered across sponsors

Solicitations, deadlines and eligibility rules live in separate portals, PDFs and spreadsheets.

Slow to read and judge

Each announcement takes real reading before anyone can say whether it fits a particular researcher.

Easy to find too late

An opportunity surfaced a week before the deadline cannot become a proposal.

How it works

AI does the first pass. People make the calls.

Every run follows the same five steps, and the last two always belong to a person.

  1. Confirm the researcherResearch areas are quoted from public sources and confirmed by a person before anything is screened.
  2. Read announcements liveCurrent opportunities are pulled at run time, then filtered to the deadline window in code.
  3. Screen with evidenceEach judgment quotes the sentence it rests on. A quote that cannot be found in its source is discarded.
  4. Review the evidenceResearch Development confirms eligibility, checks who was surfaced, and who was missed.
  5. Edit and sendA person edits the alert and sends it under their own name. SIGNAL never sends anything.

What you can rely on

Nothing is asserted without a source

  • Every claim carries its sourceEach rating quotes the exact sentence it rests on, with a link and the date the page was read.
  • Unsupported claims never helpIf a quote cannot be found in its source it is discarded, and the assessment falls back to the cautious value.
  • Eligibility is never guessedIf the announcement does not state it, SIGNAL says “Needs verification” and leaves it to a person.
  • The rating is calculatedThe model reports observations. A published rubric turns those into High, Medium or Low, and every result shows which rule produced it.
  • Dates are computed, not writtenDeadlines and the calendar are ordinary code. A missing deadline stays “Not stated”.
  • Fit is never a percentageAnd nobody is ever described as uncompetitive.
Runs are logged. Runs, inputs and calendars are logged for the preview report, kept for 90 days, then archived. Only the administrator can see them.
