About the project

What SIGNAL is, and what it is not

SIGNAL compresses the slowest part of finding funding: reading long announcements and judging whether they fit a particular researcher. It does the first pass. People make the decisions.

Scope

Two ways in, one careful process

Research development staff usually start in one of two places. Both end the same way: a person reviews the evidence and decides what goes out.

Path A · Researcher first

A researcher asks what is coming up for them.

SIGNAL starts from confirmed research areas and screens open and forecasted opportunities in the chosen deadline window.

Path B · Opportunity first

A new announcement appears.

SIGNAL reads the announcement and screens a department’s public faculty listing for people who may fit.

What it does

  • Reads current funding announcements at the moment you run it
  • Screens them against research areas a person has confirmed
  • Quotes the sentence behind every judgment, with a link and a retrieval date
  • Flags eligibility problems, and archived or replaced postings
  • Builds a deadline calendar with internal deadlines, and exports it
  • Drafts an alert for a person to edit and send

What it does not do

  • Decide eligibility. If the sources are silent it says “Needs verification”
  • Send anything. Every message is sent by a person, under their name
  • Predict whether an award is likely, or score anyone as a percentage
  • Keep a standing index of faculty. Researcher evidence is used for one run only
  • Replace the sponsor announcement, which is always the authority
  • Cover every funder. It reads selected official sources and says so

AI & human collaboration

Each does what it does best

These boundaries govern every SIGNAL workflow: the AI reads and proposes, and a person decides.

Where AI helps most

Speed, volume and first-pass analysis, where speed is of the essence.

  • Rapid synthesis: summarizing announcements, policies and reports
  • Drafting and structuring: first drafts of emails, summaries and tables
  • Pattern identification: themes across many documents
  • Consistency: shared language across communications
  • Targeted analysis: opportunity and researcher matching
  • Early sensemaking: framing questions and surfacing options quickly

Where human judgment is required

To provide trust, context and accountability.

  • Final interpretation: strategic choices, priorities and trade-offs
  • Policy and compliance: grant rules, legal and financial risk
  • Equity and ethics: who is surfaced, and who is missed
  • Institutional knowledge: culture, history and informal constraints
  • Relationships: faculty engagement and leadership communication
  • Accountability: owning outcomes in audits and reviews

Humans are essential for setting the oversight, validation of information and direction, and the ethical guardrails.

How a rating is produced

The model reports. The rubric decides.

The model never picks the rating. It reports observations, each with an exact quote. A published rubric turns those observations into a rating, and every result shows which rule fired.

Rating rules
What the sources showRating
The established science is central and nothing needs to changeHigh · Direct fit
Central, but needs sponsor-specific framing, or the researcher cannot leadHigh · Very strong fit
Partial overlap, or depends on a partner or application bridgeMedium · Strong fit
Scientifically adjacent, or a positioning rule is unmetLow · Conditional fit
No real overlap, a stretch, or a stated restriction rules it outExcluded

If a supporting quote cannot be found in its source, it is discarded and the observation falls back to its cautious value, so an unsupported claim can never raise a rating. Research Development can review and change these rules; every version is kept and each run records which one it used.

Data & privacy

What is kept, and for how long

  • No account neededNo personal data is collected from visitors.
  • Runs are loggedRuns, inputs and calendars are kept for 90 days, then archived. Only the administrator can see them.
  • Public and private data onlyNo student records, health information or financial account details.
  • No standing indexAnnouncements are read live from official sources and are not kept in a database.

Questions about research data handling go to your institution's research compliance office and your approved protocol.
